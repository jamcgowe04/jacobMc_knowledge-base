# History of Vinyl Records
______
### For over a century, vinyl records have been a physical form of media. The roots go back to when Thomas Edison invented the phonograph in 1877. This device was powered by a crank mechanism, which had to be turned manually throughout the entire recording process. Sounds like a lot of work right? Even so, it was revolutionary at the time it was invented. The technology of the phonograph laid the foundation for turntables that came after it and is still carried today through modern record players.

### After Edison's invention of the phonograph, more innovation would lay the groundwork to introduce the idea of what would become vinyl records. Emile Berliner would go on to invent the gramophone 10 years after Edison invented the phonograph. The gramophone has much more similarities to turntables that are seen today. Unlike the phonograph, it used flat discs to play recorded audio. the use of this device was strictly to play recorded music instead of recording music; making it more consumer friendly than the Phonograph. This became the standard in how vinyl records are played now.

## The Evolution of Vinyl spanned through these different eras: 

1. The Shellac Era: Early pressings on brittle resin made from shellac (not vinyl), which limited playback speed and allowed only a few minutes of audio on each side.
2. Introduction of Vinyl: Columbia Records would debut the LP and change the industry. They were made of Polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and the microgrooves inside these pressings allowed up to 20 min of audio on each side. In 1949, RCA Victor would introduce the [[Glossary|7-inch 45 rpm single]], which would then become the standard playback for individual songs.
3. Golden Age: Vinyl began to dominate the market for home entertainment. By the late 50s, stereophonic sound would get its introduction, creating a broad and communal experience in music.
4. Launch of the CD: The introduction of the compact disc (CD) resulted in a digital decline for vinyl through the 1980s and 1990s. CDs became the digital alternative and standard form of physical media to use.
5. Revival: Vinyl began to have a massive resurgence going into the 2010s. Collectors started to return to this format for its tactility and authentic sound. This would help vinyl sales climb higher to overcome digital and CD sales.
