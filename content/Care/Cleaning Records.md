# Cleaning Records

Keeping vinyl records is one of the most important and easiest ways to improve sound quality and extend the life of both the record and the stylus. Dust, fingerprints, and other debris can settle into the grooves of a record, causing pops, clicks, and distortion during playback. Over time, dirt can also increase wear on the stylus, reducing its lifespan.

For routine maintenance, many collectors use a carbon fiber brush before and after each listening session. The brush removes loose dust and helps reduce static electricity. When a record requires a deeper cleaning, a microfiber cloth and a vinyl-safe cleaning solution can be used. It is important to avoid household cleaners because they may contain chemicals damage the vinyl. 

After cleaning, records should be allowed to dry completely before being returned to their sleeves. Proper cleaning not only improves playback but also helps preserve the record's condition for years to come.

## Tips
- Brush records before every play.
- Hold records only by the edges and label.
- Never use household glass cleaners or abrasive cloths on vinyl records.
