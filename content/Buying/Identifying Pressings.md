# Identifying Pressings

Not every copy of an album is the same. Records are often produced in multiple pressings, each manufactured at different times, locations, or by different record labels. Understanding these differences helps collectors determine a record's authenticity, rarity, and potential value.

One way to identify a pressing is by examining the catalog number, label design, barcode, and matrix numbers etched into the runout groove, also known as the deadwax. These markings provide information about where and when a record was manufactured.

First pressings are often sought after because they represent the earliest production of an album. However, later pressings, remastered editions, and anniversary releases can also offer excellent sound quality. Learning how to identify pressings enables collectors to make informed purchasing decisions and better understand the history of each record.

### Information to Check

- Catalog number
- Matrix/runout numbers
- Record label
- Country of manufacture
- Release year
- Barcode (if present)
