# Grading Records

Understanding record grading is an essential skill for any collector. Grading provides a standardized way to describe the physical condition of both the vinyl and its album jacket, helping buyers know what to expect before making a purchase.

Most sellers use a grading scale that ranges from Mint (M) to Poor (P). A Mint record is considered flawless and is rarely found outside of sealed copies. Near Mint (NM) records have been played very little and show almost no signs of wear. Very Good Plus (VG+) records may have light surface marks but still play exceptionally well. Lower grades typically indicate more noticeable wear, scratches, or damage that may affect playback.

Collectors should remember that grading is somewhat subjective. When buying online, reading seller descriptions and viewing photographs can provide additional confidence before purchasing. 

### Common Grading Scale

| Grade                | Description                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Mint (M)             | Perfect condition              |
| Near Mint (NM)       | Almost flawless                |
| Very Good Plus (VG+) | Minor wear, excellent playback |
| Very Good (VG)       | Noticeable wear, plays well    |
| Good (G)             | Heavy wear and noise           |
| Poor (P)             | Significant damage             |
