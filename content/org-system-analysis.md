# Assignment 02: Analyzing an Organizing System


## The Public Knowledge base I've selected is History.com. This website carries knowledge, as well as facts on the worlds past that goes back centuries. It's an expandable knowledge base that is like an online museum that continues to record major events, and studies.

  

------------------

  

## 1. Organization Of Primary **Resources**

History.com has an organization of primary resources that consists of facts and information that relates to the worlds history. These resources include articles that covers major events that are recorded, influential figures, civilizations, arts, culture, wars, political movements, science, and important eras in history. This website also organizes multimedia resources that include documentaries, historic photos, interactive timelines, podcasts, and collections of topics that allows user to explore related subjects in more depth. 

The design of its organizing system helps users locate information by time period, geographics, subjective areas, or themes. This makes it easier to locate connections between different events and people across these historical periods. For example, if user is researching World War I, learning about ancient civilizations, or exploring technology and culture. This website provides a well structured collection and knowledge base that is sufficient in reliability of research, learning, and interests in History for students, teachers, and the general public.

  

## 2. Primary *Interactions* on History.com

The Interactions supported by History.com centers on assisting users in discovering, accessing, and exploring historical information. User who visit this website primarily interact by searching for specific topics and browse categories that are organized by time periods, articles, and multimedia resources. 

This website encourages exploration through providing links to related topics, recommended articles, and thematic collections that helps find content beyond the topic the original search that was done. History.com has educational interactions that are supported through chronological navigation, topic pages, and cross references that connect events, individuals, and places in the world across periods of history.

  

## 3. Classification of History.com

Analyzing this website further, I've found that it primarily uses a Hierarchical system to organize its content. All information is grouped into different categories that pertains to U.S History, World History, Military History, Ancient Civilizations, Crime, Arts and Entertainment, and Technology. Within these major categories that are found at the top of the homepage, is more content that is divided into subcategories that are specific to historical events, topics, and articles. 

This structure allows users to simply navigate and narrow their focus to a specific topic through the website. In addition to History.com's Hierarchical system, it limits faceted elements which includes the search function and related topic links, which helps users locate information and discover additional content to their interests. These features are supplemental however, and do not replace the implemented hierarchical structure.

  

## 4. Relations between resources (embedded content, categories, links)

The most common method of relations between resources is the use of hyperlinks that are embedded within articles, allowing users to navigate directly to other related events, historical figures, places, and concepts that are mentioned in pieces of text. In many articles at the end, History.com provides sections that feature more articles and content recommendations that encourages users to continue exploring subjects connected to what they're currently reading.

Additionally, resources are grouped into broader categories and topic pages which organize related content under a common theme. Some of these articles also include photo galleries, videos, and timelines to complement written content and gives more perspective on a topic. This web of resources structures research and learning, making it easier to discover information while understanding how historical topics can relate to each other.
